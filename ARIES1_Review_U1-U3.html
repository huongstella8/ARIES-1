📖 Unit 1 — Storytelling & Describing Objects
Narrative Tenses · Verb Patterns · Describing Possessions

Narrative Tenses — The 3 storytelling tenses

Past Simple → main completed action
She woke up early and decided to go out for a walk.
Past Continuous → background scene, ongoing action
I was driving home when my phone rang.
Past Perfect → action that happened BEFORE another past action
She had sent me a weird message that I hadn't had time to reply to.
⚠ Common error: "I am driving when…" → correct: "I was driving when…"

Verb Patterns — Verb + following form

Verb + to-infinitive: want, decide, hope, plan, promise, refuse, offer, agree, manage, learn, forget, try
She decided to leave early. / He hopes to become a doctor.
Verb + -ing: enjoy, avoid, suggest, mind, finish, keep, imagine, consider, admit, deny
I enjoy listening to podcasts. / She admitted taking the money.
Verb + preposition + -ing: (when a preposition comes before a second verb, use -ing)
I believe in telling the truth. / She succeeded in persuading people.
Stop/Remember + to-inf vs -ing (two different meanings):
She stopped to talk to me. (stop in order to do something) / She stopped talking. (stop doing something completely)
Remember to set your alarm. (don't forget to do) / I remember playing as a child. (remember having done)

except for / apart from / (not) even

except for / apart from + noun → excluding (same meaning)
I like all gadgets apart from smartwatches. / She eats everything except for onions.
even → emphasises something unexpected or unusual
I'll even stay late to help you finish it.
not even → emphasises a surprising negative
She'd changed so much I didn't even recognise her!

💼 Unit 2 — Working Life
Conditionals · Modals · Interview Language · Preferences

unless / even if / in case — Conditions & Precautions

unless = if … not (negative condition)
I can't concentrate unless it's quiet. (= if it is NOT quiet)
even if = despite the fact that (the condition does not change the result)
I keep working even if I'm tired. (Being tired doesn't stop me.)
in case = because something might happen (precaution / preparation)
I keep my phone on in case someone needs me.
⚠ Don't confuse in case with if:
✗ "I'll call you in case I have a problem." (= implying you expect a problem!)
✓ "I'll call you if I have a problem." (= only if it actually happens)

Necessity, Obligation & Permission — Modal Verbs

Necessity (something essential): need to, have to, it's essential to
I need to find my own work every week.
Obligation (required or expected): be supposed to, be expected to, be required to
Gig workers are not supposed to expect employee benefits.
Permission (something allowed): be allowed to, be permitted to, can
You're allowed to choose your own hours.
No necessity (no requirement): don't need to, don't have to
You don't have to work for the same company.

Expressing Preferences — Expressing Preferences

would prefer + to + infinitive:
I'd prefer to work outdoors.
would rather + bare infinitive (NO "to" after rather):
I'd rather work outdoors. ✗ NOT: I'd rather to work
I'd go for + noun/gerund:
I'd go for something creative.
Strong preference:
I'd much rather work from home than in an office.
I'd far prefer a creative job to a routine one.

🔍 Unit 3 — Fact or Fiction?
Past Plans & Intentions · Indirect & Negative Questions · Talking About the News

Past Plans & Intentions

was/were going to → a plan that changed or did not happen
I was going to call you, but I was too busy.
was/were planning to → a deliberate plan made in advance
She was planning to submit it early, but ran out of time.
was/were hoping to → a wish or expectation viewed from the past
He was hoping to finish by Friday, but he couldn't.
had meant to (informal) → planned but forgot or failed to do
I meant to introduce you, but he left early. / She didn't mean to cause trouble.
had intended to (formal) → deliberate purpose; common in writing
The doctor intended to see me last week, but had to cancel.
had been going to (past perfect) → plan that had been abandoned
They had been going to confess many times, but they were afraid.

Indirect Questions — More Polite Forms

After opener phrase → use affirmative word order (S + V), NO inversion:
Could you tell me… / Do you know… / Can I ask (if/whether)… / I was wondering…
Direct: Where does she work? → Indirect: Could you tell me where she works?
Direct: Did she win? → Indirect: Can I ask if she won?
⚠ Common error: "Could you tell me where does she work?" → WRONG
Correct: "Could you tell me where she works?"

Negative Questions

Checking/confirming information: Didn't / Isn't / Wasn't / Haven't + subject?
Didn't she win a BAFTA? (I think she won — confirming)
Expressing surprise:
Isn't it time to go? (I'm surprised you're not ready)
Polite persuasion: Don't you think… / Wouldn't you say…
Don't you think we should take a taxi? / Wouldn't you say this is his best film?
Answering negative questions: Use a full sentence instead of just yes/no
A: Wouldn't you like a drink? B: Thank you, that would be lovely. (NOT just "Yes.")

📖 Unit 1 — Narrative Tenses (Past Simple / Continuous / Perfect)

Past Simple — completed main action

Used for the main events of the story. Answers: "What happened?"

She woke up early and decided to go for a walk.

Past Continuous — background scene

Used for an ongoing background action interrupted by another event.

I was driving home when my phone rang.

Past Perfect — earlier past action

Used to clarify that an action happened BEFORE another past action.

When I arrived, she had already left.

Combining all three tenses in one sentence

I was driving (ongoing background) when I suddenly noticed (main event) that I had forgotten (earlier action) my wallet.

Part 1: Short questions — give 2–4 sentences per answer. Click "Model Answer" to compare.

Unit 1 — Objects & Stories
Q1: Do you have any possessions that are very special to you? Why?
🎯 Model Answer (Band 7+) ▾
Yes, I have a few. The one that comes to mind most immediately is an old camera that belonged to my grandfather. It's not worth a lot in monetary terms, but it's extremely special to me because it reminds me of spending weekends with him when I was young. The camera is a bit damaged now, and honestly I keep meaning to get it repaired — but somehow I never do. Apart from its sentimental value, it's also a genuine vintage piece, which makes it even more interesting to me.
✅ Key language: belonged to, not worth a lot, special to me, damaged, genuine, apart from, keep meaning to (verb pattern)
Unit 1 — Gadgets
Q2: What gadgets do you use every day? Could you live without them?
🎯 Model Answer (Band 7+) ▾
Apart from my phone, I'd say my wireless earphones are the gadget I use most. They're incredibly compact and portable, which means I take them everywhere. As for living without them — I wouldn't say it's impossible, but it would definitely affect my daily routine. I enjoy listening to podcasts while commuting, so I'd probably miss doing that. Actually, I think what makes modern gadgets so valuable is how multifunctional they are — my phone is a camera, a map, a bank, and an entertainment system all in one.
✅ Key language: apart from, compact, portable, enjoy + -ing, miss + -ing, multifunctional, actually
Unit 2 — Working Life
Q3: Do you prefer to work alone or in a team? Why?
🎯 Model Answer (Band 7+) ▾
Honestly, I would rather work alone for focused tasks, but I definitely prefer to collaborate when it comes to creative projects. I think I'm fairly independent by nature — I keep to a routine more easily when I'm on my own and I find I get a lot more done without interruptions. That said, I also think it's essential to have good team relationships because working in complete isolation can make you lose perspective. So even if I could choose to work alone all the time, I'd probably still want some collaborative time built in.
✅ Key language: would rather, prefer to, independent, keep to a routine, get a lot done, essential to, even if
Unit 3 — Media & News
Q4: How do you usually find out about the news?
🎯 Model Answer (Band 7+) ▾
I mainly use social media and news apps, to be honest. Apparently most people my age get their news the same way — through things that are trending or have gone viral online. The advantage is that you don't have to wait for a scheduled broadcast. Breaking news reaches you instantly. The downside, though, is that not all of it is reliable — some sources are clearly biased or present misleading information as fact. So I've started to make a point of checking stories across multiple sources before I decide to believe them.
✅ Key language: apparently, trending, gone viral, don't have to, breaking news, biased, misleading, decide to + inf

Unit 1 — Cue Card
Describe an important object you own. Say: what it is / how you got it / what it looks like / why it is important to you.
🎯 Model Answer Band 7+ (Unit 1) ▾

One object that is particularly important to me is a foldable travel backpack that I bought about two years ago before a trip to Europe. I needed something compact and lightweight, so I spent a while researching options.

In terms of appearance, it's rectangular when open but becomes very small when folded. It's made of waterproof fabric with some carbon-fibre parts on the straps, which makes it extremely durable. The texture is mostly smooth with some rubbery grip areas on the handles.

What makes it special is its multifunctional design — it has many compartments and even converts into a shoulder bag. I use it almost every week for work and short trips. Compared with conventional backpacks, this one is much more practical and portable. From a practical point of view, it helps me stay organised and travel lightly. As far as I'm concerned, it's not just a bag — it represents my love for simple and efficient living.

Unit 1 — Discussion
Do you think people buy too many unnecessary objects these days? Why / Why not?
🎯 Model Answer Band 7+ ▾
Yes, I do, and I think the evidence is fairly clear. Consider the statistic that the average American home contains 300,000 items — that's a staggering number. From my point of view, the problem is largely driven by advertising. We're persuaded to keep buying things we don't actually need. Apart from the obvious financial cost, there's also an enormous environmental impact. I think people need to stop and start thinking about whether they really need something before they buy it. That said, I understand that for some people, surrounding themselves with objects — like the maximalist designer Richard Mbuya described — brings genuine joy. It's not necessarily unhealthy to own things; the problem is when we accumulate things we don't value.
✅ Key language: consider (opener), from my point of view, apart from, need to stop + -ing, start thinking
Unit 2 — Discussion
Do you think the rise of gig work is positive or negative for society?
🎯 Model Answer Band 7+ ▾
It's a complex issue and I think the answer depends on who you ask. For young people, I'd say it's largely positive — you're allowed to choose your own hours and location, you don't have to commute, and you have a lot of independence. However, for older workers or those with families, the lack of security can be genuinely difficult. Gig workers are not supposed to receive employee benefits like sick pay or a pension, which puts them at a serious disadvantage. Even if the freedom is appealing, I think it's essential to have some form of protection in case a worker becomes ill or injured. Unless governments introduce better regulation, I fear companies will continue to exploit gig workers.
✅ Key language: allowed to, don't have to, independent, not supposed to, even if, essential to, in case, unless, exploit
