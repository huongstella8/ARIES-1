Useful Phrases for Expressing Opinions (IELTS Speaking Part 3)

Exercise 1 - Causes and Effects

Practice causal language: can lead to - can result in - can be caused by - can result from - can be affected by

Grammar tip: Use modal verbs (can, could, may, might) to avoid being too definite.
Pattern: [Effect] can result from [Cause] | [Cause] can lead to [Effect] | [Thing] can be affected by [Factor]

Exercise 2 - Collocations

Choose the adjective that does NOT collocate with the noun. Then match correct collocations.

What is a collocation? Words that naturally go together. e.g. "environmental damage" not "ecological damage". Strong collocations show high lexical resource in IELTS.

Exercise 3 - Word Forms (-ion nouns)

Complete the table and sentences using the correct verb or noun form.

Pronunciation rule: Stress in -ion nouns always falls on the syllable BEFORE -ion.
e.g. po-LU-tion | mi-GRA-tion | con-SUMP-tion | e-MIS-sion

Exercise 4 - Expressing Views and Opinions

Complete opinion phrases, match them to justifications, and classify question types.

IELTS Speaking tip: In Part 3, use: opinion phrase - reason/evidence - example or consequence. e.g. "Personally, I think governments need to do more. Unless they act, the consequences will be severe."

Exercise 5 - Vocabulary in Context

Synonyms, T/F/NG, and classification practice.
