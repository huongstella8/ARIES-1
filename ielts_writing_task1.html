IELTS Writing Task 1 — Overview

Master the fundamentals before diving into individual task types

📋 What is Writing Task 1?

RequirementDetail
Minimum words150 words
Recommended time20 minutes
Recommended length160–200 words
% of Writing score33%
TaskDescribe / summarise a visual
Do NOT includePersonal opinions, solutions, causes

⚠️ Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Writing under 150 words (automatic band reduction)
  • Describing every detail — only select key features
  • Giving opinions: "I think this is because..."
  • Writing an introduction that is too similar to the question
  • Forgetting an overview paragraph

📊 The 7 Task Types

📈 Line Graph

Shows trends over time. Uses trend vocabulary.

📊 Bar Chart

Can show trends (over time) OR comparisons (static).

🥧 Pie Chart

Shows proportions at one or two time points.

📋 Table

Data in rows/columns — static comparison or trends.

🔀 Mixed / Two Graphs

Two different graph types — describe both, link them.

⚙️ Process

Steps in a cycle or linear process. Passive voice.

🗺️ Map / Plan

Town/area before and after changes. Present perfect passive tense.

🔑 The Two Key Categories

📈 CHANGE OVER TIME (Dynamic)

Use TREND vocabulary:

  • rise, fall, increase, decrease
  • soar, plummet, fluctuate, level off
  • dramatically, gradually, significantly
  • Tense: Past simple, present perfect

Examples: Line graph, bar chart over years

📊 SINGLE TIME POINT (Static)

Use COMPARISON vocabulary:

  • higher/lower than, more/less than
  • twice as many, half as much
  • the most/least popular
  • Tense: Past simple or present simple

Examples: Pie chart, table, single-year bar chart

🏗️ Universal Essay Structure (I-O-B)

I — Introduction1 sentence
Rephrase the question
O — Overview2–3 sentences
Main trends / biggest features
B — Body Paragraphs2 paragraphs
Specific data with numbers/% & comparisons
⭐ Examiner's Tip
The Overview is the most important paragraph for a high band score. Without it, you cannot score above Band 5 for Task Achievement. The overview must identify the most striking features — not just repeat facts.

📝 Overview Practice Exercises

10 Questions
Q1 — True / False
You should write your opinion about why trends occurred in Task 1.
FALSE. Task 1 is purely descriptive. Never include opinions, reasons, or solutions.
Q2 — Multiple Choice
Which paragraph is MOST important for a high band score in Task 1?
A) Introduction   B) Overview   C) Body Paragraph 1   D) Conclusion
B) Overview. Without a clear overview, you cannot achieve above Band 5 for Task Achievement.
Q3 — Classification
Classify each graph type as (A) Dynamic / Change over Time or (B) Static / Single Time Point:
1. Line graph showing tourist numbers 2000–2020
2. Pie chart showing market share in 2023
3. Bar chart comparing 5 countries' GDP in 2025
4. Table showing how sales changed from 2010 to 2024
1. A — Dynamic (trend vocabulary)   2. B — Static (comparison vocabulary)   3. B — Static (comparison)   4. A — Dynamic (trend vocabulary)
Q4 — Error Correction
Find and correct the error: "The graph shows information about sales. I think this is because more people started using smartphones."
Remove "I think this is because more people started using smartphones." — Task 1 does NOT include opinions or reasons. The corrected version: "The graph shows information about sales." (End there or continue with descriptive details.)
Q5 — Complete the Sentence
An IELTS Writing Task 1 response must be at least _____ words and should take approximately _____ minutes.
150 words; 20 minutes.
Q6 — Overview Writing
A graph shows: UK coffee consumption rose from 2kg to 8kg per person (1990–2020). Tea fell from 10kg to 4kg. Overall coffee is now more popular. Write ONE overview sentence using this data.
Model answer: "Overall, while coffee consumption increased significantly over the period, tea drinking declined, and by 2020 coffee had become the more popular beverage."
Q7 — Identify the Error
Which part of this introduction is wrong? "The line graph shows the number of tourists who visited Spain from 2000 to 2020. In conclusion, tourism grew steadily."
"In conclusion" belongs in the Overview or a conclusion, NOT in the introduction. The introduction should only rephrase what the graph shows — no summary of results. Also, "In conclusion" is inappropriate phrasing for Task 1.
Q8 — Matching
Match each graph term with its definition:
1. Key   2. Y-axis   3. Segment   4. Row
A. Horizontal section in a table
B. Vertical measurement axis
C. A wedge-shaped section of a pie chart
D. Legend showing what colours/symbols mean
1–D  |  2–B  |  3–C  |  4–A
Q9 — Structure Ordering
Put these essay parts in the correct order for IELTS Task 1:
A) Specific data with numbers for the second group of features
B) General overview of main trends
C) Rephrase the question as an introduction
D) Specific data with numbers for the first group of features
C → B → D → A   (Introduction → Overview → Body 1 → Body 2)
Q10 — Band Score Knowledge
A student writes 148 words for Task 1. What will happen to their Task Achievement score?
Their Task Achievement score will be penalised — IELTS examiners apply an automatic penalty for falling under 150 words. The student should aim for 160–180 words to be safe.
Dynamic Graph

📈 Line Graph

Show trends over time — the most common Task 1 type

🏗️ Essay Structure

IntroductionRephrase: type of info + what/where/when
OverviewOverall trend + highest/lowest OR most striking features
Body 1First category or time period with specific data
Body 2Second category/time period with comparisons

Writing the Introduction

Rephrase the question — do NOT copy it. Change key words using synonyms.

ElementHow to Rephrase
Type of graphline graph → the graph/chart; bar chart → the diagram
"shows"illustrates, presents, compares, reveals, depicts, provides information about
"the number of"the amount of, the figure for, the quantity of, the proportion of
Time phrasebetween 2000 and 2020 → over a 20-year period / during a two-decade span
Question Task "The line graph shows the number of tourists visiting Spain from 2000 to 2020."
Introduction (rephrased) "The graph illustrates how the number of tourists travelling to Spain changed over a 20-year period from 2000 to 2020."

📖 Writing the Overview

Cover the 2–3 most important features. Do NOT use numbers. Do NOT say "In conclusion".

✅ What to include in an Overview:
  • Overall trend (rose, fell, fluctuated, remained stable)
  • Highest and/or lowest points
  • Any crossover or reversal of trends
  • Any dramatic change worth noting

Useful overview starters:

Overall, ...  |  In general, ...  |  As can be seen, ...  |  It is clear that ...

📝 Line Graph Practice Exercises

12 Questions
Q1 — Verb Selection
Choose the correct verb: Sales _____ from 200 to 800 between 2010 and 2015 — an extremely rapid change.
A) rose   B) rocketed   C) dipped   D) levelled off
B) rocketed (or soared/skyrocketed) — "extremely rapid" indicates a very dramatic rise, needing a strong verb.
Q2 — Sentence Transformation
Rewrite using adjective+noun structure: "The number of visitors declined steadily over the decade."
"There was a steady decline in the number of visitors over the decade." — OR — "The number of visitors experienced a steady decline over the decade."
Q3 — Preposition Gap-fill
Complete: "The figure rose _____ 200 _____ 450, an increase _____ 250 units."
"The figure rose from 200 to 450, an increase of 250 units."
Q4 — Introduction Rewriting
Rewrite this introduction using different vocabulary: "The line graph shows the number of cars sold in Japan from 2000 to 2020."
Model: "The graph illustrates the changes in car sales figures in Japan over a 20-year period beginning in 2000." — Key changes: "illustrates" for "shows", "changes in" added, "sales figures" for "number of cars sold", "over a 20-year period" for "from 2000 to 2020"
Q5 — Adverb Degree
Sales fell from 500 to 495. Which adverb is most appropriate?
A) dramatically   B) sharply   C) marginally   D) considerably
C) marginally (or negligibly / slightly). A drop of only 5 units from 500 is extremely small — use a weak adverb.
Q6 — Overview Writing
Data: Country A's exports rose from $10bn to $80bn (2000–2020). Country B's fell from $60bn to $20bn. They crossed in 2012. Write an overview sentence.
Model: "Overall, while Country A's exports grew dramatically over the period, those of Country B declined significantly, and the two figures crossed in 2012, after which Country A became the larger exporter."
Q7 — Time Expression
Which time expression correctly describes data from 2005 moving forward 5 years?
A) from 2005 to 2010   B) over the next five years   C) until 2005   D) Both A and B
D) Both A and B. "From 2005 to 2010" and "over the next five years" (if 2005 has been mentioned) are both correct.
Q8 — Sentence Linking
Combine these two sentences using "despite + V-ing": "Sales fell in 2010. They recovered strongly by 2015."
"Despite falling in 2010, sales recovered strongly by 2015." — OR — "Sales recovered strongly by 2015, despite having fallen in 2010."
Q9 — Projected Data
A graph shows a dotted line projecting figures to double by 2030. Choose the correct language:
A) "The figure doubled in 2030."   B) "The figure is predicted to double by 2030."   C) "The figure has doubled by 2030."
B) Use future modal language for projections: "is predicted/expected/set to..." Do not use simple past or present perfect for future projections.
Q10 — Error Correction
Find the error: "Sales reached to a peak of 500 in 2015, before declining by 2020."
Remove "to" — correct: "Sales reached a peak of 500 in 2015." ('Reach' does not need 'to'.) Also: "declining by 2020" should be "declining to [value] by 2020" or "declining until 2020".
Q11 — Grouping Data
A line graph has 4 lines: UK and France rising throughout; Germany and Italy falling throughout. How should you organise your body paragraphs?
Group by trend: Body 1 = UK and France (both rising — describe and compare them). Body 2 = Germany and Italy (both falling — describe and compare them). This shows good analytical grouping rather than describing each line separately.
Q12 — Vocabulary Range
You have used "rose" three times in your essay. Write 4 alternative verbs/phrases you can use instead.
Any four from: increased, grew, climbed, surged, shot up, jumped, soared, went up, rocketed — also consider noun forms: "there was a rise in...", "experienced an increase"

Dynamic Graph

📊 Bar Chart — Change Over Time

A bar chart showing data across multiple years — use TREND vocabulary

⚠️ How to Identify a Dynamic Bar Chart

A bar chart is "dynamic" (change over time) when:

  • The x-axis shows years / time periods (e.g., 2000, 2005, 2010, 2015)
  • The question asks you to describe changes over a period
  • You can trace a trend for each category across years
⚠️ Common Mistake
If a bar chart shows data for one year across multiple countries → use COMPARISON (static) vocabulary. If it shows data across multiple years → use TREND vocabulary.

📝 Essay Approach

The approach is identical to a line graph — I-O-B structure with trend vocabulary. The only difference is how you refer to the graph:

Introduction options "The bar chart illustrates..." / "The diagram shows..." / "The chart compares the changes in..."

Grouping Strategy for Bar Charts

SituationHow to group body paragraphs
2–3 categories, multiple yearsBody 1: categories that rose. Body 2: categories that fell.
Many years, 1 categoryBody 1: first half of period. Body 2: second half.
Two charts (e.g., two countries)Body 1: Chart 1. Body 2: Chart 2. Compare in each paragraph.

💡 Additional Language for Bar Charts

Because you can see exact values more easily in a bar chart, ensure you include:

  • Starting and ending values: "In 2000, sales stood at 200 units, rising to 450 by 2020."
  • Peak year: "Sales reached their highest point in 2015, at approximately 600 units."
  • Comparing bars: "The figure for 2020 was more than double that of 2000."
Model Body Paragraph "Turning to renewable energy consumption, solar power usage grew dramatically over the period, rising from just 5% in 2000 to 35% by 2020. Wind energy followed a similar upward trend, climbing from 8% to 28% over the same period. Both figures reached their highest points in 2020, having increased roughly four-fold over two decades."

📝 Dynamic Bar Chart Exercises

10 Questions
Q1 — Identification
A bar chart shows energy production in Germany in 2000, 2005, 2010, 2015, 2020 for coal, gas, and renewables. Is this (A) dynamic or (B) static? What vocabulary should you use?
A) Dynamic — multiple time points shown. Use TREND vocabulary: rise, fall, grow, decline, dramatic, gradual, etc.
Q2 — Introduction Writing
Write an introduction for: "The bar chart shows renewable energy consumption (solar, wind, hydro) in Germany from 2000 to 2020."
Model: "The bar chart illustrates how the consumption of three renewable energy sources — solar, wind, and hydroelectric — changed in Germany over a 20-year period from 2000 to 2020."
Q3 — Describing Dramatic Change
Solar energy: 2% in 2000, 38% in 2020. Write ONE sentence describing this change using both a verb and an adverb.
Model: "Solar energy consumption soared dramatically from just 2% in 2000 to 38% in 2020, representing a nineteen-fold increase over the period."
Q4 — Transformation: verb → noun
Rewrite: "Coal usage declined steadily from 60% to 15% between 2000 and 2020." (Use a noun form)
"There was a steady decline in coal usage, from 60% to 15% between 2000 and 2020." — OR — "Coal usage experienced a steady decline from 60% to 15% over the period."
Q5 — Grouping Decision
Bar chart data: Solar (rose throughout), Wind (rose throughout), Coal (fell throughout), Gas (fell from 2010). How would you group your body paragraphs?
Body 1: Solar and Wind (both increased — compare them). Body 2: Coal and Gas (both declined, though Gas only from 2010 — note this difference).
Q6 — Crossover Description
In 2010, solar (15%) overtook coal (12%) for the first time. Write a sentence describing this crossover.
Model: "Notably, in 2010 solar energy consumption overtook coal for the first time, standing at 15% compared to coal's 12%." — OR — "By 2010, solar had surpassed coal, reaching 15% as coal fell to 12%."
Q7 — Overview Writing
Write an overview for this data: Solar and wind both grew massively; coal and gas declined; renewables became dominant by 2020.
Model: "Overall, renewable energy sources — particularly solar and wind — grew substantially over the two decades, while fossil fuels declined considerably. By 2020, renewables had become the dominant form of energy production in Germany."
Q8 — Preposition Practice
Fill in: "Wind energy production climbed ___ 8% ___ 28%, an increase ___ 20 percentage points, ___ the period 2000 to 2020."
"Wind energy production climbed from 8% to 28%, an increase of 20 percentage points, over the period 2000 to 2020."
Q9 — Error Correction
Correct this sentence: "The bar chart shows that solar energy experienced a dramatic rise. This is because solar panels became cheaper."
Remove "This is because solar panels became cheaper." — Never explain WHY changes happened. Only describe what the data shows. Corrected: "The bar chart shows that solar energy experienced a dramatic rise."
Q10 — Complex Sentence Building
Combine into one sentence: "Hydro energy stayed roughly constant. It remained between 18% and 22%. This was throughout the period."
Model: "Hydro energy, by contrast, remained relatively stable throughout the period, fluctuating marginally between 18% and 22%."

🥧 Pie Chart

Describing proportions and comparing distributions

🏗️ Essay Structure

Pie charts are always static (show one time point OR compare two time points).

IntroductionRephrase: type of info + categories + time(s)
OverviewLargest/smallest category; any striking change (if 2 charts)
Body 1Major categories with % data & comparisons
Body 2Minor categories OR second pie chart data
📌 If you have TWO pie charts (different years):

Treat them as a dynamic task — use language like "grew from X% to Y%", "the proportion of X increased", "X's share declined". Your overview should note the biggest changes between the two.

💬 Key Language for Pie Charts

FunctionExpressions
Largest category"The largest share / segment was X, accounting for..."
"X made up the biggest proportion, at..."
"X constituted the majority, with..."
Smallest category"The smallest segment was Y, representing only..."
"Y had the least significant share at..."
Comparing two"X was twice as large as Y" / "X accounted for nearly three times as much as Y"
Combined categories"Together, X and Y made up over half..." / "Combined, these two categories represented..."
Change (2 pie charts)"The proportion of X grew/declined from...to..."
"X's share increased significantly / fell slightly"

📐 Useful Verbs for Pie Charts

Proportion verbs:

account for make up represent constitute comprise form take up

All of these mean the same thing — vary your vocabulary! "Rent accounted for 35% of expenditure." = "Rent made up 35% of expenditure." = "Rent constituted 35% of expenditure." = "35% of expenditure was comprised of rent."

📋 Table

Selecting, comparing, and organising tabular data

🔍 How to Read a Table

A table has rows (horizontal) and columns (vertical). It may show:

  • Static data (one time point — e.g., statistics for 5 countries in 2026)
  • Dynamic data (multiple years — use trend vocabulary)
⭐ Key Skill: Data Selection
Tables often have a LOT of data. Do NOT describe every cell. Select the most striking features: highest, lowest, biggest contrast, most interesting comparison. Group related data in the same paragraph.

📝 Essay Strategy for Tables

IntroductionWhat the table shows (rephrase)
Overview2–3 most striking facts, no numbers
Body 1Select highest/most notable rows or columns + compare
Body 2Remaining notable data + further comparisons

Grouping Strategies

Table TypeBest Grouping
Countries × categoriesGroup countries with similar profiles OR group by highest/lowest category
Years × categoriesTreat like a dynamic graph — group by trend
Large table (many rows)Body 1 = top 2–3 rows. Body 2 = bottom rows. Always compare.

💬 Language for Tables

FunctionPhrase
Introducing a row/column"Looking at [country/category]..." / "With regard to [transport mode]..."
Highest value"[City] recorded the highest figure for X, at..."
Lowest value"[City] had the lowest rate of X, standing at just..."
Comparing two items"In contrast to [A], [B] showed..." / "While [A] had..., [B]..."
Similarity"Both [A] and [B] had similar figures for..." / "[A] and [B] were comparable in terms of..."
Noteworthy data point"Notably, [city] was the only one to..." / "Interestingly,..."

PhraseUse

📖 Present Simple Passive — The Core Grammar

Formula: Object + is/are + past participle (+ by agent)

ActivePassive
Workers pick the leaves.The leaves are picked.

⚠️ When to use ACTIVE voice

⚡ Advanced Linking Structures

1. Once/When/After + Present Perfect

Pattern: once/when/after + subject + have/has been + past participle, ... "Once the tea leaves have been picked, they are then spread out in the sun to dry."
"After the bottles have been cleaned, they are filled with the liquid."
"When the mixture has cooled, it is poured into moulds."

2. After + Being + Past Participle (Passive -ing)

Pattern: After being + past participle, subject + is/are + past participle "After being picked, the tea leaves are put in the sun to dry."
"After being sorted, the bottles are sent to the washing facility."
"After being heated, the material is shaped by a machine."

3. Having + Past Participle

Shows that one action is completed before the next "Having been dried, the leaves are then packed into boxes."
"Having been treated, the water is safe to drink."

⚡ Band 9 Language Checklist

✅ DO

  • Vary your vocabulary — never use the same word twice
  • Use both verb forms AND noun forms
  • Group data meaningfully in body paragraphs
  • Include a clear overview without specific numbers
  • Use approximation language (about, roughly, just over)
  • Include specific data (%, figures) in body paragraphs
  • Make comparisons between categories
  • Use a range of sentence structures (simple, complex, compound)

❌ DON'T

  • Write opinions or explanations ("I think this is because...")
  • Include specific numbers in the overview
  • Describe every single data point
  • Copy the question word for word as your introduction
  • Use "In conclusion" in a Task 1 essay
  • Write under 150 words
  • Use the same word more than twice
  • Forget to compare data between categories/countries
