IELTS Writing Task 1: Maps — Complete Interactive Lesson

One map vs. multiple maps · Structure · Grammar · Vocabulary · Coherence & Cohesion · Auto-scored practice from basic to advanced

1Know the task: one map vs. multiple maps

In Task 1 you may see one map (describe a place as it is / will be) or, far more often, two or more maps of the same place at different times (describe the changes). The examiner marks you on Task Achievement, Coherence & Cohesion, Lexical Resource, and Grammatical Range & Accuracy — 25% each.

ONE mapTWO / MULTIPLE maps
What you doDescribe the location of the main features and how the place is organised (zones, layout).Describe the main changes between the time periods, and what remained the same.
Key languagePrepositions of place, there is/are, passive of location (is situated / is located).Verbs of change (demolish, construct, convert) mostly in the passive; comparison language.
Main tensePresent simple (or future passive if a planned development).Depends on the dates — see the tense decision table below.
Overview says…The general layout: what dominates, how the area is divided.The general direction of change: e.g. more urban, less rural, more developed, more modern.
🎯 Examiner insightDo not just describe the maps — describe the developments that took place. Weak answers list; strong answers select, group and compare.

Know the variants

Besides the classic pair of town maps, the same method covers: three maps (describe the two biggest stages of change; treat the middle map briefly), building / floor plans (an arts centre, hotel or school layout — same structure, with entrance, reception, corridor, at the rear of, on the opposite side of the building), one map only (describe the layout zone by zone in the present simple: the site is dominated by…, the northern half contains…), and planned developments (a map of today + an architect's plan — use future passives: will be constructed, is to be built, is due to open).

2The 4-paragraph plan (memorise this)

ParagraphWhat it containsExample opening
1 · Introduction
(1 sentence)		Paraphrase the question: what, where, when. Never copy the question word-for-word.The maps show the changes which took place in the coastal town of Youngsville between 1990 and 2010.
2 · Overview
(1–2 sentences)		The most noticeable general trend(s). No details, no numbers of items.Overall, the area was transformed from a largely rural to a mainly urban landscape.
3 · Body AChanges in one part of the map (e.g. south of the river) or one category (e.g. residential).South of the river, all of the trees were cut down and the houses along the railway were replaced by skyscrapers.
4 · Body BChanges in the other part (e.g. north of the river) or another category, plus what stayed the same.North of the river, the woodland was cleared to make way for a park and a golf course.

How to divide the map — the Method

Step 1 — Number the changes on the second map. Step 2 — Divide the town into two logical halves (north/south, east/west, or by a river/road/railway). Step 3 — Or group changes by category: residential · commercial · industrial · recreational. Step 4 — Note what did not change (churches, ponds and bridges love to survive). Step 5 — Write general statements first, then support with specific examples.

🎯 Examiner insightYou get more marks for summarising ("a range of recreational facilities was added") than for listing every change ("a pool was built, a gym was built, a cinema was built…"). Group details under headings: a swimming pool, cinema and bowling alley are all recreational facilities.
⚠ Common trapSay "on the left/right side of the map" is acceptable, but never "the left side of the town". Prefer compass directions: in the north-east of the town.

3Tense decision table — let the dates decide

The maps show…Main tensesModel sentence
Past → Past (1990 → 2010)Past simple passive; past perfect for "by + date"The forest was cut down. By 2010, the flats had been converted into a hotel.
Past → Now ("today")Present perfect passive + past simpleA new tram line has been built; a bike-rental scheme has been introduced.
Now → Future (planned)Future passive / going toA marina will be constructed at the mouth of the river.
One map, no changePresent simple (+ passive of location)The stadium is located north of the hospital.
⚠ Verbs that can NEVER be passivetake place, become, change (intransitive), make way for, appear, disappear, remain. Say: A number of changes took place — never "were taken place".
🎯 Past perfect techniqueUse By + date + past perfect to describe changes completed before a point in the past: By 2012, the block of flats had been turned into a hotel. This adds valuable grammatical range.

⚠ Collocation traps (very common errors) ① transform / convert into only work for areas or buildings that change use: "the park was transformed into an airport" ✔ (a park is an area of land) — but "the trees were transformed into an airport" ✘. Say: the trees were chopped down and replaced by an airport.
② install / establish are for facilities and systems, not houses: "an airport was established" ✔, "a house was installed" ✘.
③ demolish / knock down are for buildings; use cut down / chop down / uproot / clear for trees.

Survival & no-change language

remained (the same) · remained unchanged / untouched · very few trees remained · the area remained vacant / undeveloped · one feature which has remained the same is…

Articles: A for new, THE for old

Use a/an for things that appear on the second map (new), and the for things already known from the first map: The residential area was replaced by a warehouse.

Exercise 4 · Active or passive?

Map answers rely on the passive because we don't know (or care) who made the changes. Identify each sentence.

💡 Explanation & improvementPassive = be + past participle (was relocated, were demolished) — the subject receives the action. Active = the subject does it (they built, the university created). 💡 Explanation & improvement① transform/convert into only for areas or buildings changing use — trees can't become an airport, so: chopped down and replaced by. ② install/establish for facilities/systems, never houses. ③ Articles: a = new on map 2, the = already existed on map 1. ④ converted into (not "transformed to"). ⑤ left/right only for the map, compass points for the town. Appendix A · Two annotated model answers

introductionoverview / summarygrouped body detail

Model 1 — Past → Today (Sunnyhills University campus): present perfect & past simple mix

The two maps show changes to the campus of Sunnyhills University between 1995 and today. We can see that the university made many changes during this period, including new buildings and recreation facilities.

In the past, the Scott Library was in front of the Founder's Building. It was relocated to the right, and in addition, it increased in size. The old library building now contains laboratories. In 1995 there was a car park in the top left-hand corner of the campus, whereas now the main reception is there. The halls of residence were moved to the far left of the campus and a new Arts Building was built opposite them.

The old sports ground, gym and tennis courts were demolished; however, in their place the university created a new recreation area with a pond, trees and seating areas. What is more, the university developed new sports facilities, including table tennis tables, a new gym and squash courts. They also built two new cafés.

So, it is clear that the university changed and expanded during this period.

Why it scores: bodies grouped by theme (buildings → recreation), passive/active mix, cohesive devices (in addition, whereas, however, what is more), synonym variety (relocated/moved, built/created/developed, increased in size/expanded). Teacher’s note: the closing “So, it is clear that…” is slightly informal — in the exam prefer “Overall, …”.

Model 2 — Past → Past (Beach hotel 2003 → 2013): past simple + past perfect

The maps show changes to a beach hotel between 2003 and 2013. Generally speaking, the changes illustrate the extension and improvement of its facilities during the ten-year period.

The most striking alteration is to the hotel's capacity, with an increase in the number of rooms, and in particular the construction of an extra storey above the initial row of rooms. Another significant change is the relocation of various facilities. By 2013, the swimming pool and restaurant had been moved and its seating area had been enlarged.

Recreation had more prominence by 2013. This can be seen in the fact that the 2013 pool is considerably larger than ten years previously and a water sports centre had been built on the beach in front of the hotel. On top of this, the fence which divided the hotel from the beach had been removed by 2013, allowing free access to the beach.

To summarise, the hotel underwent an expansion of its accommodation and recreational amenities in the ten years between 2003 and 2013.

Why it scores: noun phrases (the construction of, the relocation of), past perfect with "By 2013", pronoun cohesion (its, this), an overview paraphrased — not repeated — in the summary (extension and improvement → expansion of accommodation and amenities).
