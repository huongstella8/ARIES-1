Governance & Corporate Leadership
📄 Reading: "UK companies need more effective boards of directors"
GOVERNANCE /ˈɡʌvənəns/ n [U] ▼
DefinitionThe system by which an organisation is directed and controlled — the rules, practices, and relationships that determine how decisions are made at the highest level.
quản trị (tổ chức / doanh nghiệp) — cách thức một tổ chức được điều hành và kiểm soát ở cấp cao nhất
🎯 Formal, neutral. Strongly negative with "failures of" or "poor." "Good governance" = strongly positive.
⚠️ "Governance" (cách quản trị) ≠ "government" (chính phủ). "Corporate governance" = cách điều hành công ty ở cấp HĐQT — không phải nhà nước.
Key Collocations
  • corporate governance
  • failures of governance
  • governance structure / framework
  • good / poor governance
  • strengthen governance
📖 From the Reading Passage
"After a number of serious failures of governance, companies in Britain should consider radical changes to their directors' roles."
✍️ Writing / Speaking
"Weak corporate governance was widely cited as a contributing factor to the 2008 financial crisis, prompting regulators to demand greater board accountability."
SCRUTINY /ˈskruːtɪni/ n [U] ▼
DefinitionClose and thorough examination or investigation of something, especially to detect problems or verify that it is satisfactory.
sự xem xét kỹ lưỡng; sự kiểm tra chặt chẽ; sự giám sát cẩn thận
🎯 Formal, neutral-to-negative. Implies critical, evaluative attention — not just interest.
⚠️ Often misused as "attention." Scrutiny = looking for problems. "Come under scrutiny" = bị kiểm tra vì có thể sai.
Key Collocations
  • close / intense / public scrutiny
  • come under scrutiny
  • face / withstand scrutiny
  • subject to scrutiny
  • media scrutiny
📖 From the Reading Passage
"The knock-on effect of this scrutiny has been to make the governance of companies in general an issue of intense public debate."
✍️ Writing / Speaking
"As public scrutiny of executive pay intensifies, boards are under increasing pressure to justify their compensation decisions to shareholders."
BOARD OF DIRECTORS / NON-EXECUTIVE DIRECTOR /bɔːrd/ · /nɒn ɪɡˈzekjətɪv/ n ▼
DefinitionBoard of directors: The group responsible for overseeing a company's management and strategic direction. Non-executive director (NED): A board member who provides independent oversight without being involved in daily operations.
Hội đồng quản trị — nhóm giám sát và định hướng công ty. Thành viên độc lập HĐQT — không tham gia điều hành trực tiếp.
🎯 The passage criticises the "classic model" of part-time NEDs — implies they may be too detached to be effective.
⚠️ British English: "board of directors." "Directors" alone = members of the board (NOT mid-level managers as in Vietnamese "giám đốc").
Key Collocations
  • sit on / serve on the board
  • the board voted to...
  • board meeting / decision
  • independent / non-executive director
  • part-time NED
📖 From the Reading Passage
"…calling into question the effectiveness of the classic model of corporate governance by part-time, independent non-executive directors."
✍️ Writing / Speaking
"The board of directors failed to anticipate the scale of the crisis, partly because non-executive directors lacked access to detailed financial information."
REMUNERATION /rɪˌmjuːnəˈreɪʃən/ n [U] formal ▼
DefinitionPayment or compensation received for work or services, including salary, bonuses, and benefits. Broader and more formal than "salary" alone.
thù lao; tiền lương + thưởng + phúc lợi (toàn bộ gói đãi ngộ — rộng hơn "salary")
🎯 Formal, neutral. "Executive remuneration" is often politically charged — scrutinised by shareholders.
⚠️ "Remuneration package" = tổng gói. Do NOT use in casual speech — say "pay" or "salary" instead.
Key Collocations
  • executive remuneration
  • remuneration package / committee
  • competitive / generous remuneration
  • remuneration policy
  • vote down remuneration
📖 From the Reading Passage
"Their powers to vote down executive remuneration policies increased when binding votes came into force."
✍️ Writing / Speaking
"Critics argue that the remuneration packages awarded to senior executives are often disproportionate to the company's actual performance."
TRANSPARENCY /trænsˈpærənsi/ n [U] ▼
DefinitionThe quality of being open, honest, and clear — operating in a way that allows outsiders to see how decisions are made and how the organisation functions.
tính minh bạch; sự công khai (trong cách hoạt động và ra quyết định)
🎯 Strongly positive. Its absence = distrust, scandal. Almost always paired with "accountability." Very frequent in IELTS academic writing on business and governance.
Key Collocations
  • greater / full transparency
  • promote / ensure transparency
  • transparency and accountability
  • lack of transparency
  • transparency in decision-making
📖 From the Reading Passage
"Many would argue that this is in the interest of transparency and good governance as shareholders use their muscle to pressure boards."
✍️ Writing / Speaking
"Greater transparency in executive pay decisions would help restore public trust and strengthen confidence in corporate governance."
SUSTAINABILITY /səˌsteɪnəˈbɪlɪti/ n [U] ▼
DefinitionIn business: the ability to operate successfully over the long term, considering economic, environmental, and social impacts — not just short-term profits.
tính bền vững; khả năng duy trì lâu dài (về kinh tế, môi trường và xã hội)
🎯 Strongly positive. The passage criticises boards for neglecting long-term sustainability in favour of short-term results.
⚠️ Includes BOTH environmental responsibility AND long-term business viability. Context determines which meaning is primary.
Key Collocations
  • long-term sustainability
  • sustainability and governance
  • environmental / financial sustainability
  • promote / ensure sustainability
  • sustainable development
📖 From the Reading Passage
"One of the main criticisms is that boards do not focus sufficiently on longer-term matters of strategy, sustainability and governance."
✍️ Writing / Speaking
"Companies that prioritise short-term profits at the expense of long-term sustainability risk damaging both their reputation and their ability to attract future investment."
CORPORATE ETHICS /ˈkɔːrpərɪt ˈeθɪks/ n [U] ▼
DefinitionThe set of moral principles and values that guide a company's behaviour — determining what is right and wrong in business decisions and relationships with stakeholders.
đạo đức doanh nghiệp; chuẩn mực đạo đức trong kinh doanh
🎯 Neutral but important. "A heightened awareness of corporate ethics" (from the passage) = positive development. Central to IELTS Task 2 arguments about business responsibility.
Key Collocations
  • heightened awareness of corporate ethics
  • breach / uphold corporate ethics
  • corporate ethics and social responsibility
  • ethical business practices
📖 From the Reading Passage
"The financial crisis stimulated a debate about a heightened awareness of corporate ethics."
✍️ Writing / Speaking
"A growing emphasis on corporate ethics has led consumers and investors to favour companies that demonstrate genuine social and environmental responsibility."

Business Values & Workplace Skills
🔊 Listening: "Four Business Values" (Collaboration · Industriousness · Creativity · Excellence) · Speaking tasks
COLLABORATION /kəˌlæbəˈreɪʃən/ n [U/C] ▼
DefinitionThe act of working jointly with others to achieve a shared goal, especially across organisations, teams, or disciplines.
sự hợp tác (cùng nhau làm việc để đạt mục tiêu chung)
🎯 Positive — but the listening challenges this: the speaker's team won by reducing collaboration. Valuable but not always optimal — important nuance for IELTS critical thinking.
⚠️ "Collaboration" = two separate entities working together. "Teamwork" = within one team.
Key Collocations
  • close / effective collaboration
  • in collaboration with
  • cross-departmental collaboration
  • foster / promote collaboration
📖 From the Listening
"Other teams experienced difficulties from trying to collaborate. The speaker's team won because they reduced collaboration."
✍️ Writing / Speaking
"While collaboration between departments can drive innovation, it also has the potential to slow decision-making if communication channels are poorly managed."
INDUSTRIOUSNESS /ɪnˈdʌstriəsnəs/ n [U] formal ▼
DefinitionThe quality of working hard and diligently; the habit of consistent effort and dedication.
tính chăm chỉ; sự cần cù; đức tính chịu khó làm việc
🎯 The listening complicates this: "hard work may be a bad use of company resources." Effort alone is not a virtue if misdirected — key IELTS nuance.
⚠️ Adjective: "industrious" (diligent). NOT "industrial" (relating to manufacturing).
Key Collocations
  • industrious worker / employee
  • a spirit of industriousness
  • hard work and dedication
  • diligence and effort
📖 From the Listening
"Hard work may be a bad use of various company resources. 'Lazy' in this context refers to people who avoid doing tasks that are unnecessary."
✍️ Writing / Speaking
"Industriousness is traditionally considered a core business virtue, yet research suggests that working smarter — rather than harder — often separates high-performing teams."
CREATIVITY /ˌkriːeɪˈtɪvɪti/ n [U] ▼
DefinitionThe ability to produce original and imaginative ideas, solutions, or products; the capacity to think in novel ways and generate value through innovation.
sự sáng tạo; khả năng tư duy độc đáo và tạo ra giá trị mới
🎯 Positive, but the listening notes creativity must be purposeful — "used as a response to a particular problem." Being creative for its own sake may not boost sales.
⚠️ In business: "creative solution / approach." Not "a creative" (noun) unless referring to a creative professional.
Key Collocations
  • foster / encourage / stifle creativity
  • creative thinking / solution
  • creativity and innovation
  • spark creativity
📖 From the Listening
"An advertising campaign was memorable but failed to boost sales. Creativity should be used as a response to a particular need."
✍️ Writing / Speaking
"Companies that fail to foster creativity among their employees risk falling behind competitors who are more innovative and adaptable."
EXCELLENCE /ˈeksələns/ n [U] ▼
DefinitionThe quality of being exceptionally good or outstanding; a standard of performance that significantly exceeds the norm.
sự xuất sắc; tiêu chuẩn vượt trội; chất lượng đặc biệt cao
🎯 Positive — but the listening: "companies that always aim at excellence may miss opportunities." Pioneers who act first may outperform perfectionists who wait.
⚠️ "Strive for / pursue / aim at excellence." NOT "do excellence."
Key Collocations
  • strive for / pursue excellence
  • a commitment to excellence
  • excellence in performance
  • centre of excellence
📖 From the Listening
"Pioneers had a success rate far higher than that of followers. Companies that always aim at excellence may miss opportunities."
✍️ Writing / Speaking
"While a commitment to excellence is admirable, businesses must balance the pursuit of perfection with the need to move quickly in rapidly changing markets."
WORK PLACEMENT / INTERNSHIP /wɜːrk ˈpleɪsmənt/ · /ˈɪntɜːrnʃɪp/ n [C] ▼
DefinitionA temporary position providing practical work experience. Work placement = British English (IELTS Cambridge); internship = American/global English.
thực tập (tại doanh nghiệp); kỳ thực tập; vị trí thực tập sinh
🎯 Positive. Appears in Cambridge IELTS C10, Test 4 — Laura and her tutor discuss her work placement experience.
Key Collocations
  • complete / undertake a work placement
  • paid / unpaid internship
  • work placement programme
  • gain experience through a placement
✍️ Writing / Speaking
"Completing a work placement not only equips graduates with practical skills but also helps them understand workplace culture and employer expectations."
CONFLICT RESOLUTION /ˈkɒnflɪkt ˌrezəˈluːʃən/ n [U] ▼
DefinitionThe process of finding a constructive and peaceful solution to a disagreement or dispute, especially in a workplace or organisational context.
giải quyết xung đột; hòa giải mâu thuẫn
🎯 Positive concept. Appears in Speaking Part 3 discussions about teamwork, management, and business communication.
Key Collocations
  • effective conflict resolution
  • conflict resolution skills / techniques
  • resolve a dispute
  • mediation and conflict resolution
✍️ Writing / Speaking
"Strong conflict resolution skills are increasingly valued in the modern workplace, where diverse teams must navigate differences in working style."
STAKEHOLDERS /ˈsteɪkˌhoʊldərz/ n [C, plural] ▼
DefinitionAny person, group, or organisation that has an interest in, or is affected by, the decisions of a company — including employees, customers, investors, local communities, and regulators.
các bên liên quan; tất cả những ai có lợi ích gắn với tổ chức (rộng hơn "cổ đông")
🎯 Neutral-formal. Broader than "shareholder." Implies companies have responsibilities beyond profit — to a wider community.
⚠️ CRITICAL: stakeholder ≠ shareholder. Shareholder = người SỞ HỮU cổ phần. Stakeholder = bất kỳ ai CÓ LIÊN QUAN.
Key Collocations
  • key / major stakeholders
  • engage with / consult stakeholders
  • stakeholder interests / concerns
  • stakeholder management
✍️ Writing / Speaking
"A responsible corporation proactively engages with all key stakeholders — from employees and customers to local communities — before making major strategic decisions."

Technology & Modern Work
✍️ Writing Task 2: "Business meetings increasingly online" · Speaking · HW Vocabulary List
DIGITAL TRANSFORMATION /ˈdɪdʒɪtl trænsˌfɔːrˈmeɪʃən/ n [U/C] ▼
DefinitionThe process by which a company integrates digital technology into all areas of its operations, fundamentally changing how it functions and delivers value.
chuyển đổi số — quá trình ứng dụng công nghệ số vào toàn bộ hoạt động doanh nghiệp
🎯 Strongly positive, forward-looking. Companies that fail to transform risk obsolescence. Very common in IELTS Writing Task 2 about technology and business.
Key Collocations
  • undergo / drive digital transformation
  • digital transformation strategy
  • accelerate / embrace digital transformation
  • resist digital transformation
✍️ Writing / Speaking
"Companies that fail to embrace digital transformation risk being outpaced by competitors who have integrated AI and data analytics into their core operations."
REMOTE WORK / TELECOMMUTING /rɪˈmoʊt wɜːrk/ · /ˈtelikəˌmjuːtɪŋ/ n [U] ▼
DefinitionA work arrangement where employees perform duties from a location outside the traditional office — typically home — using digital tools. Telecommuting = older/formal term; remote work = current usage.
làm việc từ xa; làm việc tại nhà
🎯 Mixed: positive (flexibility, autonomy) vs. negative (isolation, blurred boundaries). IELTS Task 2 often asks for both sides.
⚠️ "Work remotely" (adverb): "I work remotely." "Remote work" (noun): "Remote work has grown rapidly."
Key Collocations
  • work remotely / from home
  • shift to / rise of remote work
  • remote work policy / culture
  • benefits / drawbacks of remote work
✍️ Writing / Speaking
"The rapid shift to remote work during the pandemic demonstrated that many roles can be performed effectively outside the office, challenging traditional assumptions."
WORK-LIFE BALANCE /wɜːrk laɪf ˈbæləns/ n [U] ▼
DefinitionThe equilibrium between professional responsibilities and personal life — the degree to which a person can maintain a healthy division of time and energy between work and non-work activities.
cân bằng giữa công việc và cuộc sống cá nhân
🎯 Positive concept. Poor balance → stress, burnout, high staff turnover. Central to modern HR and IELTS Speaking Part 3.
Key Collocations
  • achieve / maintain / improve work-life balance
  • poor / healthy work-life balance
  • promote work-life balance
  • work-life balance challenges
✍️ Writing / Speaking
"Flexible working arrangements have been shown to significantly improve employees' work-life balance and reduce absenteeism."
AUTOMATION /ˌɔːtəˈmeɪʃən/ n [U] ▼
DefinitionThe use of technology, machines, or software to perform tasks with minimal human intervention — often replacing manual labour or repetitive processes.
tự động hóa — dùng máy móc/phần mềm thay thế lao động thủ công
🎯 Mixed. Positive: efficiency, cost reduction. Negative: job displacement, inequality. In IELTS: always present both sides.
⚠️ Automation ≠ AI. Automation = any system replacing human action. AI = intelligence-based decisions. They overlap but differ.
Key Collocations
  • industrial / office automation
  • automation of tasks / jobs
  • increased / rapid automation
  • automation threatens / creates jobs
✍️ Writing / Speaking
"While automation undeniably increases operational efficiency, its impact on employment — particularly in manufacturing — remains deeply contested."
PRODUCTIVITY /ˌprɒdʌkˈtɪvɪti/ n [U] ▼
DefinitionThe efficiency with which inputs (time, labour, capital) are converted into outputs (goods, services, results). Higher productivity = more output per unit of input.
năng suất (lao động); hiệu suất làm việc
🎯 Strongly positive. "Low productivity" = problem. Directly relevant to Writing Task 2 about online meetings — does it help or hinder productivity?
⚠️ INCREASE / BOOST / IMPROVE / REDUCE productivity. NEVER "make productivity" or "do productivity."
Key Collocations
  • increase / boost / improve productivity
  • labour / worker productivity
  • productivity levels / gains
  • measure / hamper productivity
✍️ Writing / Speaking
"Employees who work from home often report higher productivity, largely due to fewer distractions and greater autonomy over their schedule."
DISRUPTION /dɪsˈrʌpʃən/ n [U/C] ▼
DefinitionIn business: a radical innovation that displaces established industries or business models. More broadly: an interruption that prevents a process from working normally.
(kinh doanh) sự phá vỡ thị trường; đổi mới đột phá / (chung) sự gián đoạn; xáo trộn
🎯 DUAL meaning — critical distinction:
✅ POSITIVE: "disruptive innovation" / "market disruption" (e.g., Uber disrupted taxis)
❌ NEGATIVE: "disruption to services" / "supply chain disruption" (= problem)
⚠️ Context is ESSENTIAL. "Disruptive technology" = positive breakthrough. "Service disruption" = something went wrong.
Key Collocations
  • market / industry disruption (positive)
  • disruptive innovation / technology
  • cause disruption to (negative)
  • supply chain disruption
✍️ Writing / Speaking
"The rise of e-commerce has caused significant disruption to the traditional retail sector, forcing brick-and-mortar stores to rethink their business models."

Graph & Data Language
📊 Writing Task 1: "Annual earnings of three bakeries in London, 2000–2010" (Bernie's Buns · Lovely Loaves · Robbie's Bakery)
PEAKED AT / REACHED A PEAK OF /piːkt æt/ verb phrase ▼
DefinitionReached the highest point before declining or levelling off. Always followed by a figure and/or time reference.
đạt đỉnh điểm (tại mức ...); đạt mức cao nhất là ...
🎯 Neutral, precise, essential for Task 1. Implies that after this point, the figure decreased or stabilised.
⚠️ Always include "at" + figure: "peaked AT £100,000." "Reached a peak of" = same meaning, slightly more formal.
Key Collocations
  • peaked at [£X] in [year]
  • reached a peak of [figure]
  • hit a high point of
  • before peaking at
📖 Task 1 Graph Context
Bernie's Buns' annual earnings peaked at approximately £100,000 in 2003, before declining steadily over subsequent years.
FLUCTUATED /ˈflʌktʃueɪtɪd/ verb (past) ▼
DefinitionRose and fell repeatedly with no clear, consistent direction — showing irregular variation over time.
dao động (lên xuống không theo hướng nhất định); biến động thất thường
🎯 Critical distinction: "fluctuated" = irregular movement up AND down (no clear trend). Do NOT use if the line only rises or only falls.
⚠️ "Fluctuated between £X and £Y" to be precise about the range.
Key Collocations
  • fluctuated between X and Y
  • fluctuated considerably / wildly / slightly
  • fluctuations in + noun
  • show / display fluctuations
📖 Task 1 Graph Context
Lovely Loaves' income fluctuated considerably throughout the decade, with no sustained upward or downward trend.
REMAINED STABLE / PLATEAUED /rɪˈmeɪnd ˈsteɪbəl/ · /ˈplætoʊd/ verb phrase ▼
DefinitionRemained stable: Showed little or no change. Plateaued: Reached a level after growth and then stopped rising — implies a period of growth before levelling off.
duy trì ổn định / đi ngang — "plateaued" = tăng rồi dừng lại ở mức cao
🎯 Subtle difference: "remained stable" = flat throughout; "plateaued" = growth stopped. Also useful: "levelled off," "stayed relatively constant," "hovered around £X."
Key Collocations
  • remained stable / relatively stable at
  • remained relatively constant
  • plateaued at / around [figure]
  • levelled off / held steady
📖 Task 1 Graph Context
Robbie's Bakery's earnings remained relatively stable at around £20,000–£25,000 for much of the early period, before rising sharply from 2007 onwards.
DECLINED SHARPLY / GRADUALLY /dɪˈklaɪnd ˈʃɑːrpli/ verb phrase ▼
DefinitionDecreased — either rapidly (sharply / dramatically / steeply) or slowly (gradually / steadily / slightly).
giảm mạnh (sharply) / giảm dần (gradually) — adverb thay đổi tốc độ
🎯 The adverb is critical. Choose precisely based on what the graph shows.
Fast drops: sharply · dramatically · steeply · significantly
Slow drops: gradually · steadily · slightly · moderately
Key Collocations
  • declined / fell sharply / dramatically
  • declined gradually / steadily
  • dropped / dipped / plummeted
  • experienced a sharp / gradual decline
📖 Task 1 Graph Context
After reaching its peak in 2003, Bernie's Buns' income declined gradually before experiencing a sharper fall between 2006 and 2008.
SURPASSED / OVERTOOK /sərˈpæst/ · /ˌoʊvərˈtʊk/ verb (past) ▼
DefinitionSurpassed: Exceeded a specific figure or level. Overtook: Went past a competitor in terms of ranking or value.
vượt qua (một mức cụ thể) / vượt lên trên (một đối thủ)
🎯 Both describe a crossover point — essential for line graphs showing multiple series.
⚠️ "Surpassed" = vượt mức (số liệu); "overtook" = vượt đối thủ (thứ hạng).
Key Collocations
  • surpassed the [figure / record]
  • overtook [Company X] to become
  • exceeded / outpaced its rivals
📖 Task 1 Graph Context
By 2010, Robbie's Bakery had surpassed both competitors to record the highest annual earnings of the three, reaching approximately £120,000.
BY CONTRAST · IN COMPARISON · OVERALL, IT IS CLEAR THAT discourse markers ▼
DefinitionBy contrast / In comparison = introduce a contrasting trend. Overall, it is clear that = open the Overview paragraph with a summary statement.
ngược lại / để so sánh / nhìn chung, có thể thấy rõ rằng
🎯 "Overall, it is clear that..." MUST appear in your Overview paragraph — signals to the examiner that you've identified the main trends.
⚠️ Do NOT write "In conclusion" in Task 1. Correct phrase for the overview: "Overall" or "In general."
Key Collocations
  • By contrast, ... / In contrast, ...
  • In comparison, ... / Compared to ...
  • Overall, it is clear that ...
  • whereas / while (mid-sentence contrast)
📖 Task 1 Example Overview
"Overall, it is clear that all three bakeries experienced significant fluctuations, with Robbie's showing the most dramatic growth by 2010. By contrast, Bernie's Buns followed a broadly declining trend after its peak in 2003."
